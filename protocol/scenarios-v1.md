# Pilot Scenarios v1

**Status: Draft for validation.** No scenario has been approved for participant use until comprehension, ambiguity, and ethics review are complete.

Every scenario is fictional and low-stakes. Do not imply that scenario outputs constitute legal, medical, investment, or professional advice.

## General instructions

Ask participants to:

1. identify directly observed information;
2. separate observations from inferences;
3. state assumptions;
4. identify missing evidence;
5. give an overall conclusion and 0–100% confidence estimate;
6. explain what evidence would change the conclusion.

A high-quality answer may remain uncertain. Participants are not rewarded for a definitive answer.

## Scenario 1: Prospective collaborator

A fictional research equipment firm is considering a collaboration with a former aerospace components engineer named Mara Vale.

The scenario packet states:

- Mara's public profile lists 12 years in aerospace components, including five years managing a supplier-quality team.
- Two former colleagues provide mixed testimonials. One describes Mara as technically rigorous; one describes her as sometimes unwilling to challenge senior stakeholders.
- A public project page credits Mara with improving a supplier-acceptance process, but it contains no outcome metrics or independent verification.
- No public record identifies Mara's existing industry relationships.
- The firm has no prior collaboration with Mara.

Task: Decide whether the evidence supports further partnership exploration, and specify the minimum additional evidence needed before recommending a partnership.

Comprehension check:

- “Extensive technical employment history” is established.
- “Strong industry relationships” is not established.
- Mixed testimony does not prove poor judgment.

## Scenario 2: Fictional investment screen

A fictional startup, Northstar Sensors, reports strong demonstrations of a temperature-monitoring sensor.

The scenario packet states:

- Three pilot customers have requested paid trials.
- One pilot customer has paid a small deposit; the other two have not.
- Northstar reports 18% month-over-month revenue growth for the most recent quarter, but provides no transaction-level ledger.
- A market analyst estimates the addressable market at 50–90 million units annually, based on a market definition Northstar's management disputes.
- Two competitors sell similar sensors; neither has disclosed Northstar's specific accuracy advantage.

Task: Produce a simulated investment-screening view. Do not make or imply a real investment recommendation. Rank the useful next checks and identify unsupported conclusions.

Comprehension check:

- Demand interest is weaker than confirmed recurring revenue.
- Revenue growth is not independently verified.
- Market-size estimates are scenario inputs, not demonstrated sales.

## Scenario 3: Intermittent technical failure

A fictional remote weather station intermittently loses telemetry for 20–40 seconds.

The scenario packet states:

- Logs show 14 radio-reset events over 30 days.
- Ten reset events occurred when ambient temperature exceeded 35°C.
- There is no record of firmware version changes during the interval.
- On five occasions, technicians replaced an antenna cable; three of those replacements were followed by 48-hour periods without a reset.
- The device enclosure has not been opened for thermal inspection.
- The battery voltage log records only daily minimums.

Task: Rank the explanations and specify discriminating tests or measurements.

Candidate explanations must remain tied to the evidence:

- thermal effects;
- antenna or cable damage;
- firmware defects;
- power instability;
- another unobserved cause.

Comprehension check:

- Cable replacement is weak evidence for a cable defect because it was not randomized.
- Heat association is suggestive, not causal.
- Missing logs may prevent separating candidate explanations.

## Scenario 4: Preliminary scientific association

A fictional study of 2,000 adults reports that a workplace monitoring app is associated with lower perceived stress.

The scenario packet states:

- Associations are adjusted for age, occupation, and self-reported baseline stress.
- Participants chose whether to use the app.
- Loss to follow-up was 18%, with lower follow-up among participants reporting high stress.
- The app may itself provide reassurance and a structured break schedule.
- The preregistered primary outcome changed after pilot testing.
- No replication or dose-response analysis is provided.

Task: Evaluate confidence in the claimed association and in the proposed mechanism. Identify alternatives, study-quality concerns, and the evidence needed for a stronger conclusion.

Comprehension check:

- Adjustment does not remove selection bias.
- Attrition may alter estimates.
- The association does not establish that the app mechanism caused lower stress.

## Validation requirements

Before public pilot use:

- test task comprehension with at least five independent reviewers;
- ensure every task has more than one defensible interpretation;
- verify that no participant needs special domain knowledge;
- confirm that condition differences cannot be confounded by unequal prompts;
- label all outputs as simulations;
- obtain ethics review if the project is classified as human-subject research.
