# Research Note 001

## From Epistemic Governor Loops to HAM Architectures

**Status:** Unvalidated research note  
**Date:** 2026-11-23

### Summary

The original research question focused on whether a human and an AI could form a coupled feedback system more epistemically calibrated than either participant operating independently.

During theory development, the focus shifted from individual participants toward system architecture. The emerging view is that calibration may depend not only on participants but also on the structure governing interaction among them.

## Established observations

1. Human-AI interactions can exhibit confidence creep, assumption drift, and bidirectional correction events.
2. Structured protocols can separate observations, inferences, assumptions, conclusions, and confidence estimates.
3. Candidate metrics include Confidence Creep Rate (CCR), Assumption Drift Rate (ADR), Calibration Alignment Score (CAS), and Bidirectional Correction Events (BCE).
4. Human-AI evaluation often compares Human, AI, and Human + AI while rarely comparing richer interaction architectures.

## Open hypotheses

### H1

Certain interaction structures may improve epistemic calibration without substantially reducing creativity or exploratory reasoning.

### H2

Calibration may be partially determined by governance structure rather than participant capability alone.

### H3

Human-AI systems may be better modeled as architectures than as isolated agents.

### H4

Some forms of intelligence or calibration may emerge from feedback structures rather than individual participants. This is a stretch hypothesis and has no present empirical support.

## Emergence of HAM

A provisional notation is introduced:

- **H** = Human
- **A** = Artificial Intelligence
- **M** = Machine / Mechanism / Mechanized Agent

HAM describes active participants or roles inside a system rather than every artifact present in that system. The exact meaning of M remains unresolved.

## Tools versus participants

A tentative distinction:

- **Tool:** extends capability but does not independently participate in the system's decision flow.
- **Participant:** influences future system state and participates in decision flow.

Role is an architectural property. Ontology is the nature of the participating entity. The two are not assumed to be identical.

## Framework and governance

A working distinction:

- **Framework:** defines the loop
- **Governance:** defines gates within the loop

Working notation:

```text
Framework = Loop
Governance = Loop + Gates
```

Candidate gates include Observation, Assumption, Evidence, Confidence, and Correction gates.

## Open questions

1. What formally distinguishes a tool from a machine participant?
2. Can category membership change by system context?
3. Is M best defined by ontology, role, or autonomy?
4. Are framework and governance distinct constructs or levels of the same construct?
5. Can gate structures be measured empirically?
6. Does calibration arise from participants, governance, or topology?
7. Can HAM architectures outperform Human + AI pairings?

## Current position

> Epistemic calibration may be influenced by interaction architecture, and governance through explicit gates may provide a measurable mechanism for reducing confidence creep and assumption drift.

Anything stronger remains an empirical hypothesis.
